From Narratives of Value to Valuable Algorithms

A decade of promotional screen culture in the UK, seen by machine

01 · The archive

The adverts are in the recordings, but not yet in the catalogue

Since 2016 the BFI National Archive has recorded television off air from the UK's main commercial public service channels. Every advertisement broadcast on those channels is somewhere in the recordings. The adverts are not catalogued as items, however. They sit inside thousands of hours of continuous video, between and within the programmes they interrupted.

NaVVA turns this material into a collection that can be studied, and then studies it. The project treats a decade of television advertising as a record of how social and economic values were presented to audiences in the UK, and it asks what computational methods can and cannot see in that record.

02 · The question

One question, asked of ten years of commercial television

What can a computational analysis of the BFI's broadcast advertising archive reveal about the construction and negotiation of British national identity on commercial television between 2016 and 2026?

03 · The approach

Three steps, each with a different kind of reader

Archive to corpus

The corpus is made, not found

Single adverts are cut out of continuous recordings using the broadcast schedule, the soundtrack and the picture. No machine learning decides where an advert begins or ends, and every cut is logged so that it can be checked.

Machine

A model describes; it does not interpret

A vision-language model annotates each advert, for example who appears, where the scene is set, and which national markers are visible. Following Arnold and Tilton's Distant Viewing (2023), the project treats these outputs as annotations that still need interpretation.

Human

Where the model misreads is also evidence

A purposively selected set of adverts is read closely with the vocabulary of social semiotics. The places where the model's descriptions and the close readings differ are recorded and analysed, because they indicate what kind of cultural knowledge reading national identity in advertising requires.

The claims of the project are claims about the BFI collection, not about British television advertising as a whole. No archive footage is shown on this page.

05 · Status

In progress, 2025 to 2029

The project began in October 2025. Work so far has concentrated on corpus construction, that is, on cutting single adverts out of the recordings and measuring how accurate the cuts are. Further code, documentation and publications will be linked from this page as they are released.

advert-silence-boundary is the code and method documentation for the corpus construction step. It cuts single adverts out of broadcast recordings from silences in the soundtrack, the broadcast schedule and scene changes in the picture.
